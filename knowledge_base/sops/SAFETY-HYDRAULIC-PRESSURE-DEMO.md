### 1. Fluid Pressure Hazards Overview
High-pressure fluid injection represents a severe, life-threatening hazard. Hydraulic systems operating above 50 bar can penetrate human skin instantly, creating catastrophic deep-tissue chemical injuries requiring emergency surgical intervention. Pneumatic lines carry severe whip and projectile hazards.

### 2. General Depressurization Guidelines
1. **Never Inspect with Bare Hands:** Never use fingers or bare hands to feel for pinhole hydraulic leaks. Always use cardboard, wood, or an indirect acoustic/infrared detector.
2. **Pneumatic Isolation & Exhaust:** Close primary pneumatic ball valve and lock in the exhausted/vented position. Confirm pressure gauge drops to 0.0 bar.
3. **Hydraulic Accumulator Bleed-Down:** Open manual accumulator depressurization needle valves. Verify accumulator pressure gauge reads zero bar.
4. **Mechanical Ram Locking:** For hydraulic stamping presses (e.g., M102), mechanically insert certified safety die blocks beneath the upper platen before working inside the die space.
5. **Residual Energy Trapping:** Be aware that counterbalance valves can trap high pressure between valve and cylinder port even when the central pump is de-energized.
